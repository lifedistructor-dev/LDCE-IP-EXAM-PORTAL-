1. Introduction — Money Laundering & Terrorism Financing परिचय — धनशोधन एवं आतंकवाद वित्तपोषण

🔴 VIMP🟡 Concept
▶
Chapter: Introduction→Sub: Definitions→Micro: 3 Stages→Nano: Placement/Layering/Integration
Definition — Money Laundering

Process of converting "dirty" money (from illegitimate sources) into "clean" money that appears legitimate. अवैध रूप से अर्जित धन को वैध रूप देने की प्रक्रिया को धनशोधन (Money Laundering) कहते हैं।

3 Stages of Money Laundering 🔴

1. Placement

Physical disposal of illegal cash proceeds.
अवैध नकदी का भौतिक निपटान

➜

2. Layering

Complex financial transactions to hide the audit trail.
जटिल लेनदेन द्वारा स्रोत छुपाना

➜

3. Integration

Laundered money re-enters economy as "normal" business funds.
धन को वैध व्यवसाय के रूप में अर्थव्यवस्था में वापस लाना

🧠 Mnemonic

P-L-I → "Please Launder Immediately" 😄

Placement → Layering → Integration (order matters for exam MCQs!)

Definition — Financing of Terrorism

Transactions involving funds (owned or not by a terrorist) used, or intended to be used, to assist commission of terrorism. ऐसे धन का लेन-देन जो आतंकवादी गतिविधियों में प्रयोग हेतु हो, चाहे वह धन आतंकवादी का हो या न हो।

  • Providing/collecting property for an act of terrorism — आतंकवाद हेतु संपत्ति एकत्र करना
  • Providing services for terrorism purposes — आतंकवाद हेतु सेवाएं देना
  • Arranging retention/control of terrorist property — आतंकवादी संपत्ति का नियंत्रण
  • Dealing with terrorist property — आतंकवादी संपत्ति से लेनदेन
❓ Probable Question: "What are the three stages of money laundering? Name them in order." / "Define Financing of Terrorism as per the Anti-Money Laundering Act."

2 & 3. Objective of AML/KYC/CFT & Definition of "Customer" उद्देश्य एवं "ग्राहक" की परिभाषा

🟠 Frequent🟢 Quick
▶
Objective

To prevent money laundering / terrorist financing through cross-border inward remittance (MTSS) or forex purchase/sale, and help Post Offices know their customers & manage risk prudently. MTSS व विदेशी मुद्रा लेनदेन के माध्यम से धनशोधन/आतंकवाद वित्तपोषण को रोकना ही मुख्य उद्देश्य है।

Who is a "Customer" under KYC (for MTSS & Money Exchange) 🟠
  • Individual visiting PO for inward remittance (occasional/regular), or for sale/purchase of forex notes/TCs — प्रेषण प्राप्त करने वाला व्यक्ति
  • Individual on whose behalf transaction is undertaken (beneficial owner) — लाभकारी स्वामी
  • An entity having business relationship with the Post Office — डाकघर से व्यावसायिक संबंध रखने वाली संस्था
❓ Who qualifies as a "customer" under KYC norms for MTSS and Money Exchange? List all three categories.

4. Customer Due Diligence (CDD) ग्राहक सम्यक तत्परता

🔴 VIMP
▶

CDD means the Post Office obtains satisfactory evidence & establishes identity/legal existence of any person doing business (remittance/forex) with it, substantiated by reliable independent documents. CDD का अर्थ है ग्राहक की पहचान व वैधानिक अस्तित्व को विश्वसनीय दस्तावेज़ों से स्थापित करना।

When must CDD be conducted? (4.1) 🔴
  • Establishing any business relationship — व्यावसायिक संबंध स्थापित करते समय
  • Any suspicion of money laundering/terrorism financing — संदेह होने पर
  • Doubt about veracity/adequacy of earlier information — पूर्व जानकारी पर संदेह होने पर
CDD must at least comprise
  • Identify & verify the customer
  • Identify & verify beneficial ownership/control
  • Obtain purpose & intended nature of relationship
  • Conduct ongoing due diligence & scrutiny
🔴 Exam Trap: Unwillingness of customer to provide info/cooperate = itself a factor of suspicion. Non-compliance → PO must terminate relationship + file STR.

5. KYC Policy — Acceptance, Risk Categorization, Documentation, CIP KYC नीति

🔴 VIMP🟠 Frequent
▶
Chapter: KYC Policy→5.1 Acceptance→5.2 CIP→5.3 Business Procedure
5.1(a) Customer Acceptance — Prohibited/Watch Situations 🔴
  • No anonymous / fictitious / benami transactions — गुमनाम/काल्पनिक नाम से लेनदेन नहीं
  • Only personal remittances allowed (family maintenance, foreign tourists) — non-related persons need close monitoring
  • No commercial/investment/loan/charity/donation/property/NRE-credit transactions under MTSS
  • No transactions in the name of minors
  • Watch: unusually frequent receipts, receiver using multiple IDs, forged IDs, no apparent family relation between sender-receiver, same person receiving from multiple senders (or vice-versa)
❓ List any five situations under Customer Acceptance Policy that require enhanced monitoring / are not acceptable.
5.1(b) Risk Categorization — LEVEL I / II / III 🔴 (High-weight table)
LevelRemittance AmountForex Purchase Amount
Level Iनिम्न जोखिमUp to ₹50,000Less than ₹50,000
Level IIमध्यम जोखिमAbove ₹50,000 up to ₹1,00,000In excess of ₹50,000
Level IIIउच्च जोखिमFamily maintenance remittance above ₹1,00,000 sent by family member abroad; foreign tourists—
Level III — Other High-Risk Triggers (memorize as a checklist) 🔴
  • Collection from a different city than the photo ID city
  • No apparent family relation between sender & receiver
  • Transactions from FATF high-risk countries (list below)
  • Sender has foreign name, receiver is Indian national
  • Same person receiving from multiple senders / same sender to multiple receivers
  • Non-resident customers; countries not applying FATF standards
  • High Net-worth Individuals (HNI); Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)
  • Non-face-to-face customers; dubious public reputation
  • Intentional structuring/breaking of transactions below ₹50,000 to evade reporting (संरचनाकरण)

🧠 Mnemonic for Level III triggers

"Ph.D. HiNe P-PEP"

Photo-city mismatch, High-risk country, Non-family, HNI, Non-face-to-face, PEP, Political... — build your own chain linking to these 9 points!

5.1(c) Documentation Requirement
  • ID proof + Address proof required for every transaction
  • Originals personally checked; photograph matched with customer
  • Single document (Voter ID/Ration Card/Passport/DL/Aadhaar) with photo + current address = valid for both ID & address
  • Foreign tourists: passport copy = ID + address; visa copy (Indian Immigration stamped) also kept; if no address on passport → passport + visa + signed declaration of permanent address
  • Level III (high risk): additional ID & address proof required over and above normal proof
Acceptable ID / Address Proof Documents 🟠
For Identity (photo)For Address
Passport, PAN Card, Voter ID, Driving Licence (Indian, not learner's/foreign), Govt/Armed Forces/PSU employee ID (not police/army IDs for civilians), Letter from recognized public authority, Aadhaar (UIDAI) card (आवेदन-रसीद मान्य नहीं) Telephone bill (<3 months), Bank a/c statement, Letter from public authority, Electricity bill (<3 months), Ration Card, Employer letter (subject to Postmaster's satisfaction)
❓ Is a "letter of acknowledgement for having applied for Aadhaar" acceptable as ID proof? Answer: No. Only the actual UIDAI Aadhaar card is accepted.
Special Case — Relatives living together

If utility bills aren't in the applicant's name (e.g., wife/son/daughter/parent living with family), Postmaster may accept the relative's ID + utility bill + a declaration from the relative confirming relationship & co-residence. परिवार के साथ रहने वाले सदस्यों हेतु रिश्तेदार का पहचान पत्र व घोषणा-पत्र स्वीकार्य।

Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) 🔴

Individuals entrusted with prominent public functions in a foreign country — Heads of State/Govt, senior politicians, judicial/military officers, senior PSU executives, political party officials.

  • Gather sufficient info + check public domain info
  • Verify identity + ascertain source of wealth & source of funds
  • Decision to accept a PEP → taken at senior level
  • Subject to enhanced ongoing monitoring
  • Norms also apply to family members/close relatives of PEPs, and to customers who become PEPs later, and where PEP is the ultimate beneficial owner
5.2 Customer Identification Procedure (CIP)

Identifying customer + verifying identity using reliable independent documents/data. Risk-based approach avoids disproportionate cost/burden. Periodic updation of ID data (incl. photographs) needed for continuing relationships.

Business Entities (company/firm/trust)

Relationship established only after due diligence on documents; ongoing due diligence throughout relationship; if CDD becomes impossible → terminate relationship + file STR with FIU-IND.

5.3 Procedure for Undertaking Business (Payment / Purchase / Sale) व्यवसाय हेतु प्रक्रिया

🔴 VIMP
▶
5.3.1 Payment to Beneficiaries under Money Transfer
  • ID docs verified + copy retained with legible photo of beneficiary
  • Biometric identification also required for cash payment to beneficiary (to be linked to UID)
  • Cap: US $2500 per individual remittance under the scheme
  • Cash payment: up to ₹50,000; above → by cheque/DD/PO or credited directly to bank a/c (exception: foreign tourist may get higher cash in exceptional cases)
  • Max 30 remittances receivable by one individual per calendar year
5.3.2 Purchase of Forex from Customers
  • < ₹50,000: photocopy of ID not required, but full details must be maintained
  • ≥ ₹50,000: ID document verified + copy retained
  • Suspected structuring into series below ₹50,000 → verify identity/address + consider STR to FIU-IND
Payment to customer for purchase of forex (cash limits)
CategoryMax Cash (INR) per transaction
Resident customerUS $1000 equivalent
Foreign visitor / NRIUS $3000 equivalent

All purchases within 30 days of last transaction = treated as a single transaction (for limits & reporting). Otherwise → payment only by Account Payee cheque/DD.

5.3.3 Sale of Forex to Customers
  • Passport mandatory for ID — irrespective of amount; only on personal application
  • Payment > ₹50,000 → crossed cheque (sponsor's bank a/c) / Banker's cheque / Pay Order / DD / debit-credit-prepaid card (subject to conditions)
  • 30-day window rule applies here too; 2nd/subsequent drawal exceeding ₹50,000 cumulative → same restricted payment modes
  • Encashment Certificate to be insisted upon, wherever required
  • Currency Declaration Form (CDF) production insisted if forex tendered exceeds prescribed limits

5.4 Monitoring of Transactions & 5.5 Risk Management निगरानी एवं जोखिम प्रबंधन

🟠 Frequent
▶

Ongoing monitoring is essential to KYC. Special attention to complex/unusually large/no-apparent-purpose transactions. High-risk txns need intensified monitoring. "Attempted transactions" abandoned by customer must still be reported as STR.

5.5 Risk Management — Key Controls 🔴
  • Level III: additional Photo ID — must be a valid Passport or UID/Aadhaar card (not a letter)
  • Level III: additional Address Proof — latest landline/mobile/electricity bill
  • Doubtful transactions (documents complete but raises suspicion): payment via Account Payee cheque for ₹20,000 and above
  • Original ID/Address proof checked & compared with copies for all transactions
  • Signature on ID proof must match signature on forms
  • All documents self-attested by customer
  • Photo on ID matched with the actual person transacting
  • Any doubt-clarifying questions must be recorded on the documents as proof
🔴 Exam Trap: Doubtful-transaction cheque threshold is ₹20,000 — different from the ₹50,000 Level I/II threshold. Don't confuse the two!

6–8. Inspection/Audit, New Technologies & Combating Financing of Terrorism निरीक्षण, नई तकनीक एवं आतंकवाद वित्तपोषण नियंत्रण

🟡 Concept
▶
6. Inspection / Audit

Circles must ensure regular auditing for AML/KYC compliance; Inspectorial staff must be well-versed and give on-hand training; lapses reported by concurrent auditors must be escalated immediately to higher authorities.

7. New Technologies

PO must watch for ML threats from new/developing technologies (e.g. internet transactions favouring anonymity) and take preventive measures.

8. Combating Financing of Terrorism (CFT)
  • Suspicious transaction = includes proceeds of an offence regardless of value — enhanced monitoring + swift STR to FIU-IND on priority
  • Countries with deficient AML/CFT regime (per FATF) — see Section 8(b) list above — special attention required

9–12. Records — Maintenance, Suspicious Transaction Definition, Preservation अभिलेख — रखरखाव, संदिग्ध लेनदेन की परिभाषा, संरक्षण

🔴 VIMP
▶
9. Records to be Maintained
  • All cash transactions > ₹10 lakh
  • Series of cash transactions < ₹10 lakh but integrally connected within 1 month & totalling > ₹10 lakh
  • Transactions with forged/counterfeit currency, or forgery of documents/security
  • Attempted transactions involving forged currency/documents
  • All suspicious transactions (cash or otherwise)
10. Definition of "Suspicious Transaction" 🔴 (verbatim-style, high MCQ value)

A transaction (incl. an attempted one, cash or not) which to a person acting in good faith:

  • (i) Gives reasonable ground of suspicion of proceeds of an offence, regardless of value
  • (ii) Appears made in circumstances of unusual/unjustified complexity
  • (iii) Appears to have no economic rationale or bonafide purpose
  • (iv) Gives reasonable ground of suspicion of terrorism-financing activity

'Transaction' = exchange/transfer of funds in any currency, cash/cheque/PO/other instruments/electronic or non-physical means.

11. Information to be Preserved
  • Nature of transaction
  • Amount & currency
  • Date of transaction
  • Parties to the transaction
12. Maintenance & Preservation of Records 🔴
Record TypePreservation Period
All transaction records (residents & non-residents)10 years from date of transaction
Customer identification records (ID/address proof copies)10 years from date of cessation of business relationship
🔴 Exam Trap: Two different "10-year" start points — transaction records count from the transaction date, but ID records count from cessation of relationship. A very common MCQ confusion.

13. Reporting of Transactions — CTR & STR Schedule लेनदेन की रिपोर्टिंग — CTR व STR समय-सारणी

🔴 VIMP🟠 Frequent
▶
Transactions to be Reported
  • (a) Cash transactions > ₹10 lakh
  • (b) Series of connected cash transactions < ₹10 lakh (within 1 month) totalling > ₹10 lakh
  • (c) Forged/counterfeit currency accepted, or forgery of security/documents
  • (d) Attempted transactions with forged currency/documents
  • (e) All suspicious transactions (domestic + international) — irrespective of amount
⏱ Reporting Timeline — Cash Transaction Report (CTR)
Day 3

Post Office In-charge → Head of Division

Monthly list of qualifying cash transactions, by 3rd working day of the subsequent month.

Day 5

Head of Division → Head of Circle

Post-office-wise consolidated list, by 5th working day of the subsequent month.

Day 8

Head of Circle → DDG (PCO), PMLA, Directorate

Consolidated post-office-wise list, by 8th working day. NIL report mandatory if no transactions.

⏱ Reporting Timeline — Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) 🔴
Same Day

Post Office In-charge → Head of Division (by name)

List with nature, amount, name/address, date/place, PAN (if given), and detailed reason of suspicion.

Same Day

Head of Division → Head of Circle (by name)

On the very same day of receiving STR from PO.

Same Day

Head of Circle → DDG (Public Compliance Officer), PMLA (by name)

On the very same day of receiving STR from Divisional Office.

🧠 Mnemonic — CTR vs STR

CTR = "3-5-8, Wait a Bit" (working days)  |  STR = "Same Day, No Delay"
❓ Compare the reporting schedule and escalation chain of a CTR and an STR. Who is the final recipient authority in each case?
Note 1: Abandoned/aborted transactions are still reportable as STR, irrespective of amount.
Note 2: Deliberate splitting of amount to avoid CTR reporting must be reported as an STR.

14–18. Attestation, Comparison, Recording & Training सत्यापन, तुलना, अभिलेखन एवं प्रशिक्षण

🟢 Quick
▶
14. Attestation of KYC Documents

All KYC documents must be self-attested. For illiterate customers: attested by Gazetted Officer / Sarpanch (Gram Panchayat) / any Postal Staff / GDS after comparing with original. PM/SPM/Supervisor (APM/DPM) must ensure attestation is done.

15 & 16. Comparison of Address & Name
  • Address in Booking/Receiving form must match address proof document
  • Name of sender/receiver in form must match Identity proof document
17. Recording Receipt of KYC Documents

PM/SPM/Supervisor records in writing, under dated signature, on the Booking/Receiving form: "KYC Documents verified & attached."

18. Customer Education & Employees' Training
  • Customer Education: Circles to prepare literature/pamphlets explaining KYC objectives; front-desk staff trained to handle customer queries
  • Employees' Training: Ongoing training programme; different focus for frontline staff, compliance staff, staff dealing with new customers; steps on suspicious-transaction handling to be formulated by Circles
Final Responsibility Clause

PO must identify/assess ML/TF risk for customers, countries, geographical areas, products/services/delivery channels, and adopt enhanced measures for medium/high-risk categories. Postmasters are solely responsible for ensuring their location adheres to these guidelines.
