Definition परिभाषा

The PMLA (Amendment) Act, 2012 (No. 2 of 2013) further amends the Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002 to widen definitions, introduce a new "reporting entity" concept (replacing "banking company, financial institution or intermediary"), enable freezing of property (not just attachment), and strengthen confiscation/recovery mechanisms.

🔒 Attachment, Seizure & Freezing of Property कुर्की, जब्ती व फ्रीजिंग Sec 5, 8, 17, 18, 20, 21

▼
Provisional Attachment — Sec 5(1) 🔴

Director/officer ≥ rank of Deputy Director may, on written reasons, provisionally attach property for a period not exceeding 180 days from date of order.

🧠 Mnemonic: "180 = 6 months of Attachment" → provisional attachment max period.
NEW Concept: "Freezing" (in addition to Attachment/Seizure) 🔴

This amendment introduces freezing as a third mechanism (Sec 17(1A)) — where property/record cannot be physically seized, an officer may order it frozen (not transferable) instead.

Attachment (Sec 5)

Prevents transfer, but ownership/possession may remain undisturbed. Max 180 days.

Freezing (Sec 17(1A)) 🔴

Used when seizure NOT practicable — property cannot be transferred without prior permission of the ordering officer.
Retention of Property — Sec 20 🔴
  • Seized/frozen property may be retained/continue frozen for max 180 days for adjudication under Sec 8
  • On expiry, property returned unless Adjudicating Authority permits continuation
  • After confiscation order (Sec 8(5)/(7)): non-involved property released; Director may withhold release for 90 days if relevant to appeal 🟠
Retention of Records — Sec 21 🟠

Similar scheme: max 180 days retention/freezing of seized records; person from whom seized is entitled to obtain copies.

Time Limit — Application for Retention/Continuation — Sec 17(4) 🔴

Authority must file application before Adjudicating Authority within 30 days of seizure/freezing.

30 Days
File application for retention/continuation of freezing (Sec 17(4))
180 Days
Max period for provisional attachment (Sec 5), retention of seized/frozen property (Sec 20) and records (Sec 21)
90 Days
Max period Director may withhold release of property/records post-confiscation-order, if relevant for appeal (Sec 20(6), 21(6))
🧠 Mnemonic: "30-180-90" → File in 30 days, hold for 180 days, withhold-post-release for 90 days.
Probable Q: Within how many days must an application for retention of seized property be filed before the Adjudicating Authority? → 30 days 🔴

🏦 Reporting Entity — Records & Obligations रिपोर्टिंग इकाई के दायित्व Sec 12, 12A, 13, 14, 15

▼
Section 12 — Duties of Every Reporting Entity 🔴
  • (a) Maintain record of all transactions
  • (b) Furnish info to Director within prescribed time
  • (c) Verify identity of clients
  • (d) Identify the beneficial owner, if any
  • (e) Maintain records of client identity documents & correspondence
Record Retention Time Limits 🔴
Record TypeRetention Period
Transaction records — Sec 12(1)(a)5 years from date of transaction
Client identity/correspondence — Sec 12(1)(e)5 years after business relationship ends/account closed, whichever is later
🧠 Mnemonic: "5-5 Record Rule" → both transaction records and identity records kept for 5 years (measured from different trigger points).
Section 12A — NEW: Access to Information 🔴

Director may call for any Sec 12(1) records + additional info from any reporting entity, for purposes of the Act. Info kept confidential.

Section 13 — Powers of Director on Non-Compliance 🟠
  • (a) Issue written warning
  • (b) Direct compliance with specific instructions
  • (c) Direct periodic reports on measures taken
  • (d) Impose monetary penalty: not less than ₹10,000, up to ₹1,00,000 per failure 🔴

Director may also order a special audit by a chartered accountant (Sec 13(1A)/(1B)) — expenses borne by Central Government.

Section 14 — Immunity 🟡

Reporting entity, its directors & employees have no civil/criminal liability for furnishing information under Sec 12(1)(b).

Probable Q: What is the penalty range under Sec 13(2)(d) for non-compliance by a reporting entity? → ₹10,000 to ₹1,00,000 per failure 🔴

⚖️ Confiscation & Release of Property जब्ती एवं संपत्ति की रिहाई Sec 8, 58A, 58B, 60

▼
Section 8(5)-(7) — Trial Outcomes 🔴
What does the Special Court conclude on trial?
Money-laundering PROVEN → Property confiscated to Central Government [Sec 8(5)]
NOT proven / not involved → Property released to entitled person [Sec 8(6)]
Trial cannot be concluded (death/absconding of accused) → Special Court passes appropriate confiscation/release order on application [Sec 8(7)]
Sections 58A & 58B — NEW: Foreign Trial Linkage 🔴
  • 58A: If a foreign court finds money-laundering did NOT occur / property not involved, Indian Special Court may order release of the property in India
  • 58B: Where foreign trial cannot conclude, Central Govt forwards letter of request to Director → moves application before Special Court for appropriate confiscation/release order
🧠 Mnemonic: "58A Releases, 58B Requests" → 58A is about releasing property based on foreign court finding; 58B is about acting on a foreign request when their trial can't conclude.
Probable Q: What happens to property if the Special Court concludes the offence of money-laundering was NOT committed? → Released to the person entitled to receive it [Sec 8(6)] 🔴

⚖️ Burden of Proof सबूत का भार Sec 24

▼
Section 24 — Substituted (Important Rule) 🔴

(a) Person charged under Sec 3

Authority/Court shall PRESUME proceeds of crime are involved in money-laundering, unless the contrary is proved — mandatory presumption 🔴

(b) Any other person

Authority/Court MAY presume proceeds of crime are involved — discretionary presumption
🧠 Mnemonic: "SHALL for accused, MAY for others" → burden is reverse/mandatory for the accused himself, but discretionary for third parties.
Probable Q: Under Sec 24(a), is the presumption of guilt mandatory or discretionary for the person charged under Sec 3? → Mandatory ("shall presume") 🔴

👤 Key Authorities मुख्य प्राधिकरण Sec 44, 54

▼
Sec 44 — Special Court Jurisdiction 🔴
  • Offence under Sec 4 + connected scheduled offence → tried by Special Court of the area where committed
  • Special Court can take cognizance of Sec 3 offence without accused being committed for trial
  • If a different court took cognizance of the scheduled offence, that case is committed to the Special Court
  • Trial follows Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (as for Sessions trial)
Sec 54 — "Authorities" for Assistance — NEW Additions 🔴

Officers/members of these bodies are now bound to assist under PMLA:

IRDA officers
Forward Markets Commission officers
PFRDA officers
Dept of Posts officers
Registrars/Sub-Registrars (State)
Motor vehicle registering authority
ICAI officers/members
ICWAI (Cost Accountants) officers
ICSI (Company Secretaries) officers
🧠 Mnemonic: "I-F-P-D-R-M-C-C-C" → IRDA, FMC, PFRDA, DoP, Registrars, Motor Vehicle authority, CA/CWA/CS institutes — 9 new categories under Sec 54.
Probable Q: Are officers of the Department of Posts included among authorities bound to assist under Sec 54? → Yes (newly added clause (he)) 🔴

💰 Penalty & Recovery शास्ति एवं वसूली Sec 63, 69

▼
Section 63(4) — NEW Provision 🔴

Person who intentionally disobeys any direction under Sec 50 (summons/evidence powers) is also liable under Section 174, Indian Penal Code (non-attendance in obedience to summons).

Section 69 — Recovery of Fine/Penalty — Substituted 🔴
Fine/penalty (Sec 13 or Sec 63) not paid within 6 months of imposition
↓
Director/authorised officer may recover as prescribed in Schedule II, Income-tax Act 1961
↓
Officer gets powers of a Tax Recovery Officer for this purpose
🧠 Mnemonic: "6-Month Grace, Then IT-Act Recovery" → penalty unpaid for 6 months triggers Income Tax Act-style recovery.
Probable Q: Within how many months must fine/penalty be paid before recovery proceedings under Schedule II of IT Act 1961 begin? → 6 months 🔴
